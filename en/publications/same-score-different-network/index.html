Same score, different network

Same score, different network: configuration-level heterogeneity in composite risk measures (Applied Network Science, 2026).

Composite risk instruments summarize many indicators in a single severity score. This is efficient for case management, but it hides how risk factors are organized and the fact that the same total score can be reached through different paths. This article proposes a network-based framework to study that heterogeneity among cases with equivalent scores.

Data

The study uses 17,272 administrative assessments from the first module of the Intervention Management Instrument (IGI, Instrumento de Gestión de Intervención), administered by Chile’s prison service (Gendarmería de Chile) between 2015 and 2022 as part of in-prison case management. The IGI measures criminogenic needs through 43 items organized in eight domains, based on the risk–need–responsivity model.

Methodology

  1. Score windows: narrow, overlapping windows of the total score are analyzed, so that severity is held approximately constant.
  2. Configurations: within each window, item profiles are grouped by clustering with the Jaccard distance, and the stability of those configurations across windows is assessed.
  3. Ising networks: an Ising network is estimated for each configuration, describing which items tend to occur together.
  4. Robustness and outcomes: edge stability is assessed by bootstrap (B = 1000), and logistic models relate co-presence patterns to two-year recidivism.

Main results

Presence probability of each IGI item (rows) by total score (columns).

  • Identical scores correspond to distinct, recurrent network architectures: relational structure contains relevant information that the additive score does not capture.
  • Some configurations are “fragmented”, with dispersed needs weakly connected across domains; others are “coherent”, with tight co-presence among related items.
  • Within the same severity band, certain patterns of item co-presence or separation are associated with observed recidivism.
  • The results are robust to window width and to excluding the items used to define the windows.

Item co-presence and recidivism

Odds ratios (log scale) for two-year recidivism for pairs of items connected in the Ising networks, by score window (columns) and configuration (Networks 1–3), adjusting for age, sex, relationship status and mental health (M3 model). Green marks estimates significant at the 5% level. Pairs marked with † indicate co-absence; the rest, co-presence. Domains: HD, criminal history; EDU, education and employment; PAR, peers; CAD, attitude and behavior; PRO, pro-criminal orientation; UTL, leisure time use; PAT, offending patterns.

The figure shows that some item pairs with relevant partial correlations in the Ising networks help explain recidivism among people with comparable scores. The signals are not global: they appear in specific windows and configurations, and several recur across adjacent windows. For example, in Network 1 the co-presence of two attitude and behavior items (CAD32–CAD34) is associated with higher recidivism in the highest score windows, while the co-presence of CAD32 with an offending-pattern item (PAT43) is associated with lower recidivism in the intermediate windows. That these associations persist after adjusting for basic individual characteristics suggests that the way factors combine carries information the total score does not capture.

Beyond the prison case, the framework shows how network psychometrics can reveal latent structural heterogeneity in applied measurement instruments and in other score-based administrative systems.
